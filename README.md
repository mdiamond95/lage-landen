# Lage Landen

A Duolingo-style web app for learning the history and culture of the Netherlands and Belgium, built around maps, pictures and timelines.

Phase 1 from the project plan (Units 1 to 5) is built, Phase 2 (Units 6 to 9) is built, and the Netherlands track is built with Units 10 to 12. Playable now:

- Unit 1, Land and water: five lessons and a checkpoint, with a dike slider and an animated map of the Zuiderzee and Delta Works.
- Unit 2, Celts, Romans and Franks (57 BCE to 900): six lessons and a checkpoint, opening with an animated map of the Roman frontier and roads.
- Unit 3, Counts, bishops and cloth cities (900 to 1384): six lessons and a checkpoint, built around an animated map of the wool and cloth trade across the North Sea.
- Unit 4, The Burgundian Netherlands (1384 to 1555): six lessons and a checkpoint, built around a map of the lands joining Burgundy one by one, with a family tree from the dukes to Charles V and detail hunts in Flemish paintings.
- Unit 5, Revolt and the great split (1555 to 1648): six lessons and a checkpoint, built around a front-line map of the Eighty Years' War that shows refugees streaming north from Antwerp, with a population chart of Antwerp and Amsterdam and quotes from the Wilhelmus, the Union of Utrecht and the Act of Abjuration.
- Unit 6, Golden Age and Spanish Netherlands (1588 to 1700): six lessons and a checkpoint, built around a map of Amsterdam growing ring by ring on today's canals and a map of France taking the southern towns, with zoomable paintings by Rembrandt, Vermeer and Rubens.
- Unit 7, Trade, empire and slavery (1600 to 1863): six lessons and a checkpoint, built around a world map of VOC and WIC posts and ship routes, with the routes that carried enslaved Africans in their own colour. The lessons on slavery and conquest open with a content note and run in a calm mode, and the last lesson weighs two voices on the apology of 2022.
- Unit 8, Disaster year to Waterloo (1672 to 1815): six lessons and a checkpoint, built around a border-change map that steps year by year from the French invasion of 1672 through the Austrian Netherlands and the French years to Waterloo, with find-the-year questions on it in four lessons and two voices on whether Holland should stay a country of its own.
- Unit 9, One kingdom, then two (1815 to 1839): six lessons and a checkpoint on the United Kingdom of the Netherlands, the Belgian Revolution and the Treaty of London, built around a kingdom map that splits in 1830 and is settled in 1839, and a before-and-after swipe between 1815 and 1839.
- Unit 10, Constitution and pillars (1848 to 1914): six lessons and a checkpoint on Thorbecke's constitution, the colonial profits from Java and Max Havelaar, the school struggle and the pillars, workers and women fighting for the vote, railways and science, and Van Gogh and the painters of his time. Built around a diagram of the four pillars and a map of railways and canals connecting the country, with a chart of who could vote.
- Unit 11, Occupation and liberation (1914 to 1949): six lessons and a checkpoint on neutrality in the First World War, the five days of May 1940 and the bombing of Rotterdam, the persecution of the Jews and Anne Frank, hiding, resistance and the Hunger Winter, the liberation with the Canadians at the front, and Indonesia's war of independence. Built around a map that moves month by month from May 1940 to May 1945, freeing the country stage by stage. The lessons on the Holocaust and on the war in Indonesia open with a content note.
- Unit 12, Water, welfare and tolerance (1932 to today): six lessons and a checkpoint on the Afsluitdijk, the Depression, emigration to Canada and Drees's welfare state, the flood of 1953, the Delta Works, the pillars crumbling and Provo, Suriname's independence and the Caribbean islands, and coffeeshops, euthanasia, same-sex marriage and Srebrenica. Built around a close-up map of the southwest delta that floods in 1953 and closes its sea arms one by one to 1997, and a storm barrier slider. The lesson that ends with Srebrenica opens with a content note.
- Unit 13, Industry and empire (1835 to today): six lessons and a checkpoint on the first train on the continent and Belgium's state railways, the coal country of Wallonia (Grand-Hornu, Van Gogh in the Borinage, the strikes of 1886 and 1893, Marcinelle and the Italian miners), Leopold II's Congo Free State, the Belgian Congo, independence and the murder of Lumumba, and the arguments since over statues, the AfricaMuseum and apologies. Built around a railway and coal map of Belgium from 1835 to 1914 and a map of the Congo from 1876 to 1961 on its own Central Africa base. The three Congo lessons open with a content note.
- Unit 14, Flanders Fields (1914 to 1951): six lessons and a checkpoint on the German invasion of 1914 and the "scrap of paper", the civilians shot at Dinant and the burning of Leuven, the flooded Yser and Queen Elisabeth at De Panne, the Christmas truce, the first gas attack at Ypres and John McCrae's In Flanders Fields, Passchendaele and the Canadians under Currie, the war cemeteries and the Last Post at the Menin Gate, the eighteen days of May 1940, the resistance and the deportations from Mechelen, the Battle of the Bulge, and the Royal Question to 1951. Built around a trench-line map of the Westhoek, month by month from October 1914 to the Armistice, with a layer of war cemeteries and memorials, and a map of Belgium in the First World War. Two lessons open with a content note.
- Unit 15, Language and the federal state (1830 to today): six lessons and a checkpoint on the Flemish Movement, Coucke and Goethals, Coremans and the Equality Law of 1898, Destrée's letter to the king, Ghent University in Dutch, the language census and the marches on Brussels, the language border of 1963 with Voeren and the towns with facilities, the strike of 1960–61, Leuven Vlaams and Louvain-la-Neuve, the six state reforms and Dehaene's federal state, the 541 days, Expo 58 and the Atomium, Spaak, the European Commission and NATO in Brussels, the German-speaking Community, Brussels-Halle-Vilvoorde and Bye Bye Belgium. Built around a language map on a new map of Belgium, from 1830 to 2012, and a diagram of the three regions and three communities.
- Unit 16, Flemish Primitives to Bruegel (1420 to 1569): six lessons and a checkpoint on the Ghent Altarpiece and its adventures, Dürer's journey of 1520–21, Campin's Merode Altarpiece, Rogier van der Weyden, Bouts's Last Supper, Hugo van der Goes and the Portinari Altarpiece, Memling's Ursula shrine, Petrus Christus, Gerard David's Cambyses, Bosch and the Garden of Earthly Delights, Antwerp's art market with Metsys, Patinir, Gossaert and Catharina van Hemessen, and Bruegel's proverbs, winters, peasants and Icarus. Built around painting tours that glide from detail to detail in four great paintings, and a map of the painters' workshops from 1420 to 1569.
- Unit 17, Rembrandt, Vermeer, Rubens (1585 to 1682): six lessons and a checkpoint on Rubens's workshop and his paradise painted with Jan Brueghel, Van Dyck and the English court, the Utrecht Caravaggists and The Procuress, Rembrandt's light, his self-portraits, bankruptcy and the Syndics, Frans Hals, Vermeer's Art of Painting, the Little Street and the Cupid found under paint in Dresden, Van Meegeren's forgeries, the art market Evelyn saw at Rotterdam in 1641, still lifes, Steen, Saenredam and Rachel Ruysch, Avercamp's winters, Ruisdael, Hobbema and the Van de Veldes. Built around paintings side by side, two masters compared detail by detail, and a map of the Golden Age painters' workshops from the fall of Antwerp to 1682.
- Unit 18, Modern art (1880 to 1973): six lessons and a checkpoint on Van Gogh's ten years, from Sorrow and Mauve's lessons to Japanese prints, Arles, Gauguin, the ear and Auvers; Ensor's masks and Christ's Entry into Brussels, Les XX, Seurat's dots in Brussels, Anna Boch, Khnopff, Spilliaert and Rik Wouters; Mondrian's road from a red tree to a grid, De Stijl, Van Doesburg's diagonal and Rietveld's chair; Magritte's pipe and Escher's tiles, told through the men and their places as their works are still in copyright; and the collectors and museums, from Helene Kröller-Müller to the Van Gogh Museum. Built around a painter's road (one painter's pictures on a slider), a style sorter and a map of the modern artists' workshops, villages and museums.
- Unit 19, Architecture (1188 to 1996): six lessons and a checkpoint on the belfries and cloth halls of Bruges, Ghent, Tournai, Ypres and Brussels (and the legends of the Ghent dragon and the Brussels architect's leap); the cathedrals of Utrecht and Mechelen, a Baroque and a Protestant church, and the béguinages; the gables of Amsterdam's canal houses; the mills of Kinderdijk, the wind sawmill and the Wouda steam pumping station; Victor Horta's Art Nouveau in Brussels; and the modern buildings from Het Schip and the Schröder house to the Van Nelle factory, the rebuilt Rotterdam and the Atomium. Built around building fronts with their parts named on hotspots, label-the-parts questions, a map of the buildings of the Low Countries and a style sorter for buildings.
- Unit 20, Languages (about 500 to today): seven lessons and a checkpoint on the oldest written Dutch, from the Frankish law glosses and Hebban olla vogala to Reynaert the Fox, Jacob van Maerlant and the States Bible; one language in two countries, the soft g, Flemish words and the Taalunie; the dialect map, with Limburgish tones and listening questions; Frisian, Kneppelfreed and Fedde Schurer; Walloon, Belgian French, Tchantchès, French Flanders and the German speakers of the east; Dutch around the world, from Afrikaans, Papiamento and Sranan to English words of Dutch origin and nearly a million Dutch Canadians; and everyday phrases. Built around a clickable dialect map with recordings of real speakers, listen-and-find questions on it, a 100-phrase phrasebook and a map of the languages from about 800 to the regional languages recognised in the 1990s.
- Unit 21, Food and drink (1266 to today): six lessons and a checkpoint on cheese country, from the Alkmaar carriers' guild of 1593, Clara Peeters's cheeses and the front of the Gouda weigh house to Edam, Leiden cheese with its keys and Herve; herring and the sea, Beukelszoon, gibbed herring, Temple's herring placarts, mussels from Zeeland and the shrimp fishers on horseback; spices, syrup and sprinkles, from the VOC and the conquest of Banda to speculaas, stroopwafels, hagelslag and the rijsttafel; frites, waffles and chocolate, from Bruegel's Carnival and Lent to Van Houten's cocoa press, Neuhaus's praline and the myth of the Meuse fries; beer and jenever, the Trappist abbeys, lambic, Heineken, Schiedam's distilleries and Dutch courage; and the everyday table, from The Potato Eaters and the hutspot of Leiden to the Hunger Winter, Cuyp's oliebollen and FEBO. Built around a regional food map of thirty foods and drinks with photos and recordings, questions that ask where a food comes from or which items fit, a painting tour of Carnival and Lent, and a food map from 1266 to Belgian beer culture on UNESCO's list in 2016.
- Unit 22, Feasts and traditions (1303 to today): six lessons and a checkpoint on a year of feasts, from Saint Martin's lanterns, the Easter fires and Steen's bean king to the days off in each country, 4 May and Leiden's 3 October; Sinterklaas, the bishop of Myra, Steen's Feast of Saint Nicholas, Jan Schenkman's steamboat of 1850 and the debate over Zwarte Piet; carnival, the Gilles of Binche and their oranges, the Inca legend, Maastricht and the removal of Aalst from UNESCO's list; processions and giants, Isabella's Ommegang of 1615, Dürer at Antwerp, the Holy Blood of Bruges, the Doudou of Mons, Goliath of Ath and the Meyboom; King's Day and the national days, from Wilhelmina's Princess's Day of 1885 and Steen's Prince's Day to the vrijmarkt, 21 July and the King's Feast; and Keti Koti and the days of freedom, from herheri and the Keti Koti Tables to the Liberation Fire and the Remembrance of the Dead. Built around a calendar wheel of the year with 27 feasts, when-is-it questions answered by turning the wheel to a date, a painting tour of Isabella's Ommegang and a feast map from 1303 to the king's apology for slavery in 2023.
- Unit 23, Sport (1625 to 2017): six lessons and a checkpoint on a country of bicycles, from Stop de Kindermoord and the car-free Sundays to the Hovenring, Jan Janssen and Joop Zoetemelk; the Tour of Flanders over the Oude Kwaremont, the Paterberg and the Koppenberg; Eddy Merckx, the classics, Marianne Vos and the Van der Poels; Total Football, Ajax, Cruijff and Michels, Munich 1974 and Hamburg 1988; the Games of Antwerp 1920 and Amsterdam 1928, Fanny Blankers-Koen, korfball and the Heysel disaster; and Avercamp's ice, kolf and the Elfstedentocht, from the hell of '63 to the last one in 1997. Built around route maps of the classic races, stepped climb by climb with each climb's length and gradient, ride-the-route questions that tap the places in order, a sport map from 1890 to 2017 and a painting tour of Avercamp's Winter Landscape.
- Unit 24, Comics and pop culture (1929 to 2021): six lessons and a checkpoint on Tintin and the clear line, from Le Petit Vingtième, Tintin in the Congo and Le Soir under the occupation to the Moon rocket and the Hergé Museum; Spirou and the Marcinelle style, Franquin's Gaston and Marsupilami, Lucky Luke's straw and Peyo's Smurfs; Suske en Wiske, Nero's waffles and the statues of Middelkerke; Nijntje, Toonder's words, Joost Swarte's klare lijn and the Donald Duck weekly; Adolphe Sax, Django Reinhardt, Toots Thielemans, Brel's flat land, five Dutch wins at Eurovision and Tomorrowland; and Audrey Hepburn, Turks Fruit, three Dutch Oscars, Rosetta, Jeanne Dielman and Big Brother. Built around comic panel questions that put balloons in their panels or panels in order, comic strips cut from photos and paintings, the murals of the Brussels comic book route and a pop culture map from 1929 to 2021.
- Unit 25, Thinkers and scientists (1425 to 1931): six lessons and a checkpoint on Erasmus, In Praise of Folly, his quarrel with Luther over free will, Utopia from Dirk Martens's press, the Collegium Trilingue and Plantin's presses; Vesalius's Fabrica, Galen's apes, Boerhaave and Leiden's anatomy theatre; Mercator's map for sailors, Ortelius's first atlas, the king who gave the Atlas its name and Vermeer's Geographer; Stevin's decimals and sailing carriage, the Delft tower experiment, Huygens's ring of Saturn and Snellius's triangles; Leeuwenhoek's lenses, Swammerdam's mayfly, Clusius's tulips, Teylers and Eisinga's planetarium; and Lorentz, helium in Leiden, the Solvay conference of 1927, Lemaître's primeval atom, Quetelet's index, Otlet's cards, Eijkman's vitamin and Baekeland's plastic. Built around a Mercator projection explorer (drag countries to another latitude, switch to an equal-area map or a globe, Tissot's circles and a steady compass course beside the shortest way), true-size questions, and a map of universities, printers and laboratories from 1425 to 1931.
- Unit 26, How the countries work today (1839 to today): six lessons and a checkpoint on two kings who are never crowned, the Belgian oath and who answers for the king; both lower houses of 150 seats after their last elections, compulsory voting, the red pencil, the Dutch record for forming a cabinet and how provincial parliaments elect the Dutch Senate; the twelve Dutch provinces, Holland and Flevoland; Belgium's ten provinces, three regions and the split of Brabant; the Benelux, the treaties of Rome, Schengen and Maastricht, the 2005 referendum and NATO's Dutch and Belgian secretaries general; and Amsterdam, The Hague and Brussels, the water boards and the border houses of Baarle. Built around a province explorer and a map drill (find each province by name, capital or flag, also on its own screen with best scores), seat charts of both parliaments, and a map of provinces and seats of power from 1839 to today.

Around the units:

- Unit 0, a placement test for learners who know some of this already: pick a track, then answer five questions a unit, one from each of five lessons, in course order. Four right (the plan's 80% bar) opens the whole unit, every lesson and the checkpoint; after a miss the learner can stop or try the next unit anyway. Misses are not asked again, and the answers go into the daily review.
- A mastery gate: a unit opens once the checkpoint of the unit before it is passed with 8 of 10 right first time. History runs as one trunk to Unit 9 and then forks into the Dutch (10 to 12) and Belgian (13 to 15) tracks, both opened by Unit 9; culture (16 to 26) is a track of its own, open from the start. A unit the placement test opened counts as mastered, so the one after it opens too, and a unit already started stays open, as does any checkpoint finished before the gate.
- Crowns: every lesson has five levels. Finishing it is level 1; playing it again goes up a level with 80% right first time: level 2 is the lesson once more, stories and all, level 3 its questions alone in a new order, and level 4 the same with no second chance at a miss. Level 5 is the unit's Legendary challenge, opened once every lesson is at level 4: 15 questions from across the unit against a clock (25 to 60 seconds a question, running only while a question is open), passed with 12 right in time, which turns every crown in the unit gold. Lessons with a content note are never put on the clock, so Unit 7, most of whose lessons are about slavery and conquest, has no Legendary challenge.
- A museum of 495 cards: the artworks, people, buildings and food the lessons show, each hung on the learner's wall when a lesson that shows it is finished, in one room per unit, with filters by kind and a full view with the photo's caption and credit. A face asked about in a Who am I? waits for that lesson, so the wall never names it first. People cards leave out the perpetrators the course covers, and buildings leave out memorials, cemeteries and sites of persecution.
- Daily quests: three small goals a day, one warm-up (earn 30 XP, finish a lesson, find 5 places on the map), one steady (15 answers right first time, a lesson with no mistakes, 3 new museum cards, 10 questions in the review) and one stretch (60 XP, 2 lessons, a crown raised), each worth 10 XP once met. The day picks them, so everyone has the same ones, and only from what the learner can do: no review quest without 10 questions due, no crown before a lesson is finished.
- A daily review: every question in a finished lesson comes back on a spaced schedule, missed ones sooner, ten at a time.
- A time map of 47 snapshots, from Caesar's conquest in 57 BCE to the provinces and seats of power of today, built from the unit maps.
- A map drill of the 22 provinces of both countries and Brussels, by name, capital or flag, with the best score for each kept on the device.
- A phrasebook of 100 everyday phrases in Dutch, Flemish and Belgian French, in ten sets, to browse or search; 28 are recorded by speakers on Wikimedia Commons, and the rest use the device's own voice where it has one.
- XP, a day streak with one free missed day a week, and a map of the places discovered.
- Works offline once loaded, and installs on a phone's home screen.

The live site for testers is published to GitHub Pages from `main` by `.github/workflows/pages.yml`, at https://mdiamond95.github.io/lage-landen/ once Pages is switched on (Settings > Pages > Source: GitHub Actions).

## Run it

```sh
npm install
npm run dev          # local dev server
npm test             # unit tests and content checks
npm run e2e          # plays every lesson in a headless browser
npm run build        # static site in dist/
npm run build:single # one self-contained HTML file in dist-single/
```

## How it fits together

- `src/content/*.yaml`: lessons, one file per unit, checked against `src/content/schema.ts`. `npm test` fails if a lesson points at a place, province or river that doesn't exist, or has too few visual questions.
- `src/content/places.ts`: every place a lesson can pin.
- `src/content/course.ts` gives the unit before each one (`previousUnit`), and `src/progress/path.ts` decides from it which units are open (`unitOpen`, `unitMastered`); a checkpoint's best `score` is kept in its `completed` entry. Browser tests that start on a later unit call `openUnit` first.
- `src/content/levels.ts`: how a lesson plays at each crown level, and the Legendary challenge with its clock. `src/progress/crowns.ts` says what a play did to the crowns; a lesson's level is kept in its `completed` entry and passed Legendary units in `legendary`.
- `src/data/collection.ts`: the museum's cards, a title and a line for each photo. `src/progress/collection.ts` works out which lessons hang each card (`hangCards`, built once as `wall` in `src/content/wall.ts`), and `src/screens/Museum.tsx` is the wall. A food on the food map counts for a lesson whose map opens on its kind, or whose taste question asks about that kind.
- `src/progress/quests.ts`: the daily quests, how a day's three are picked and how a session counts towards them. The day's picks and counts are kept in the progress as `quests`; `ensureQuests` picks them (with `canToday` from `src/content/today.ts`) and `recordSession` counts each session's `kind`, XP, places, first-try answers, new museum cards and crowns, adding the bonus.
- `src/content/placement.ts` and `src/screens/Placement.tsx`: the placement test. `buildPlacement` draws one question from each of five lessons of a unit (no stories); units opened by it are kept in the progress as `placed`, and `src/progress/path.ts` decides which lessons and checkpoints are open. A unit's `note` opens its placement block as well as its checkpoint, so it must not name either.
- `src/data/lowlands.topo.json`: countries, provinces, rivers and the IJsselmeer from [Natural Earth](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/) (public domain), clipped and simplified with mapshaper.
- `src/map/`: the SVG map (d3-geo, Mercator), used for pins, region painting and story maps. `TimeMap.tsx` adds a year slider and a play button on top of it, drawing any `TimeStory` (lines, sites, labels and areas that were still sea, each with the years it existed). `TradeMap.tsx` is a wider North Sea map (England to the Ems, down to the Champagne fairs) with moving trade routes; both share `TimeControls.tsx` and `usePlayback.ts`.
- `src/data/northsea.topo.json`: land and main rivers for the trade map, from Natural Earth 10m, clipped to the North Sea frame and simplified with mapshaper.
- `src/data/water.ts`: the Zuiderzee works and the Delta Works for Unit 1, 1916 to 1997. Flevoland's province shape is cut into its three polders, which show as sea until they fall dry. The flood of 1953 (flooded land and dike breaches) comes from the delta map.
- `src/data/delta.ts`: the southwest delta for `{ kind: time-map, map: delta }`, 1952 to 1997, drawn close up on `DeltaBase.tsx` (the story sets `base: 'delta'`). Its land is CBS's generalised provinces (via PDOK, CC BY 4.0) with today's Delta dams cut out, and Belgium from Natural Earth 10m, in `delta.topo.json`, clipped and simplified with mapshaper; areas with `regions` draw on these detailed provinces. The land flooded in 1953 is areas of kind `flooded`, dike breaches are sites of kind `breach`, and each dam or barrier is a line from the year it closed.
- `src/data/industry.ts`: Belgium's railways and coal country for `{ kind: time-map, map: industry }`, 1835 to 1914: the state's cross of lines out of Mechelen, the lines to France and Prussia, the private lines of the 1850s and 1860s, the coal belt from the Borinage to Liège (an area of kind `coal`) and its collieries and ironworks (sites of kind `mine`).
- `src/data/congo.ts`: the Congo for `{ kind: time-map, map: congo }`, 1876 to 1961, drawn on `AfricaBase.tsx` (the story sets `base: 'africa'`): some of the peoples of the Congo basin, Stanley's journey, the Congo Free State, the Belgian Congo, Ruanda-Urundi, independence and Katanga's breakaway. Its land, lakes and rivers come from Natural Earth 50m in `congo.topo.json`, clipped and simplified with mapshaper; areas with `regions` use its country codes (COD, RWA, BDI).
- `src/art/StormBarrier.tsx`: a cross-section of the Oosterschelde barrier (`{ kind: storm-barrier }`): raise the storm surge and the gates drop at 3 metres above NAP; take the barrier away and the polder behind the dike floods.
- `src/data/roman.ts`: the Roman frontier, roads, forts, towns and captions for the time map, each with the years it existed. Lessons can also draw these lines on an ordinary map with `lines: [limes]`.
- `src/data/burgundy.ts`: the lands joining the dukes of Burgundy and then Charles V, 1384 to 1555, for `{ kind: time-map, map: burgundy }`. Each territory is drawn with the modern provinces closest to it (`regions`), cut by a rough `clip` polygon where only part of one belongs; the map says its borders are approximate.
- `src/data/revolt.ts`: the revolt map for `{ kind: time-map, map: revolt }`, 1555 to 1648. Each land changes sides with `sides(...)`: the king's (`ruled`), the rebels' (`rebel`) or split between them (`contested`, hatched). Refugee routes are lines of kind `flow` (arrows with moving dashes), and battles and sieges are sites of kind `battle`. The story sets `fresh: 'outline'`, so lands that just changed sides are outlined rather than refilled.
- `src/data/amsterdam.ts`: Amsterdam's growth for `{ kind: time-map, map: amsterdam }`, 1550 to 1700, drawn on `AmsterdamBase.tsx` instead of the Low Countries (the story sets `base: 'amsterdam'`). Its water is today's canals, the IJ and the Amstel from the Kadaster's TOP10NL (via the PDOK API, CC BY 4.0) in `amsterdam.topo.json`. Each expansion is an area of kind `city`, listed newest first so only the newest ring shows as just added; walls are lines of kind `wall`.
- `src/data/spanish.ts`: the Republic and the Spanish Netherlands for `{ kind: time-map, map: spanish }`, 1648 to 1700, with the towns France took as areas of kind `france`.
- `src/data/empire.ts`: the VOC and WIC across the world for `{ kind: time-map, map: empire }`, 1602 to 1863, drawn on `WorldBase.tsx` (the story sets `base: 'world'`; Equal Earth projection, centred on the Cape). Land is Natural Earth 50m (public domain) in `world.topo.json`, clipped to the Caribbean–Japan frame and simplified with mapshaper. Posts are sites of kind `voc` or `wic`; ship routes are `flow` lines with a `tone` (`asia`, `atlantic` or `enslaved`), each with its own arrowhead and key colour. Colonies are areas of kind `ruled` drawn from a rough `shape`.
- `src/data/borders.ts`: the borders of the Low Countries for `{ kind: time-map, map: borders }`, 1672 to 1815, built with `sides(...)` from the revolt map and the regions of the Burgundy and Spanish maps. Two kinds of area join the others: `austria` for the Austrian Netherlands from 1714 and `sister` for the Batavian Republic and Kingdom of Holland, French client states from 1795 to 1810. France's conquests are `france`, lands held by allied armies are `contested`, and the Water Line, William's fleet of 1688 and the Prussian march of 1787 are lines. Barrier fortresses (1715 to 1781) are sites of kind `fort`.
- `src/data/kingdom.ts`: the United Kingdom of the Netherlands and its split, for `{ kind: time-map, map: kingdom }`, 1815 to 1839. Two kinds of area join the others: `belgium` from 1830 and `duchy` for Luxembourg, the king's grand duchy; half of Limburg and half of Luxembourg go back to the king in 1839. New canals are lines of kind `canal`, the Wellington Barrier forts (1816 to 1829) and the forts the Dutch held after 1830 are sites of kind `fort`.
- `src/data/rails.ts`: railways and canals for `{ kind: time-map, map: rails }`, 1839 to 1914, with lines of kind `rail`, the North Sea Canal and the Nieuwe Waterweg as `canal`, the Haarlemmermeer as a hand-drawn `shape` of sea until it is drained in 1852, and Luxembourg leaving the Dutch crown in 1890. By 1914 the coal mines of South Limburg are working: the coalfield is an area of kind `coal`, the mines sites of kind `mine`.
- `src/data/occupation.ts`: the Second World War for `{ kind: time-map, map: occupation }`, May 1940 to May 1945. This map counts in months, not years (`months: true`): `month(1944, 9)` in `timeline.ts` is September 1944, the slider steps month by month and is labelled Month, and lessons write a month as `'1944-09'` (in `year`, a compare's `years`, and a scrub's `start` and `answer`). Land is `occupied` or `free`, with the Ardennes `contested` in the Battle of the Bulge; land freed in the stage on screen is outlined. Nazi camps are sites of kind `camp`, and the food drops of April 1945 are sites of kind `drop`. The Canadian war cemeteries in Belgium and the Netherlands (sites of kind `grave`) appear with the liberation. For the time map through all eras, `occupationByYear` pins its stages to 1940, 1944 and 1945.
- `src/data/greatwar.ts`: Belgium in the First World War for `{ kind: time-map, map: greatwar }`, August 1914 to November 1918, counted in months: the invasion, the towns where civilians were shot, the fall of Antwerp, occupied Belgium behind the Western Front (a line of kind `front`), the electric fence on the Dutch border (a `barrier`), and the liberation of 1918. For the time map through all eras, `greatWarByYear` pins its 1915 stage to 1915.
- `src/data/westhoek.ts`: the front around Ypres and on the Yser for `{ kind: time-map, map: westhoek }`, October 1914 to November 1918, month by month, drawn close up on `WesthoekBase.tsx` (the story sets `base: 'westhoek'`). Its land is Belgium and France from Natural Earth 10m, and its waterways (the Yser and the canals to Ypres and Dunkirk) come from the Flemish hydrographic atlas (VHA, via Digitaal Vlaanderen), in `westhoek.topo.json`, clipped and simplified with mapshaper. Each front line is a line of kind `front`, the flooded Yser plain an area of kind `flooded`, and the war cemeteries and memorials sites of kind `grave` and `memorial`; those laid out after the war appear with the Armistice.

## Writing a lesson

Add an entry under `lessons:` in a unit's YAML. Start with a `story`, then mix question types; each lesson needs at least six questions, half of them visual, and at least one source.

## Unit checklist

Each new unit is held to what the best units so far do, and when one is finished the earlier units are reviewed against it (see the retrospective notes in each PR). `npm test` enforces the first two, and requires a fill-the-gap, a cause and effect, an odd one out, a sort into groups and a before-and-after map in every unit (Unit 3, whose cloth map draws routes rather than borders, wipes across the buildings map instead).

- At least 70% of the unit's questions are answered on, or show, a map or picture, and every lesson at least half.
- At least one "Who am I?" and true or myth questions that show a picture or map.
- Paintings and old pictures are worked with, not just shown: a detail hunt where a picture has a telling detail, and find-the-year questions on the unit's time map in at least two lessons.
- A signature interactive piece: a slider or a year-slider map, reused in later questions with `year`, and a before-and-after swipe between two of its years.
- Myths and legends taught as such: a true or myth that tests a popular story against the evidence.
- Primary sources read, not just cited: a fill-the-gap question on a quote from a document, song or letter of the time.
- Things compared: an odd one out with a picture for each choice, and a reason to pick, and a sort into groups (by side, maker, era or country), with pictures on the cards where they help.
- Causes linked to effects, and numbers read from a real chart where the unit has figures worth showing.
- Every claim fact-checked against a source listed on the lesson, with figures kept consistent across units.
- Real photos, paintings and maps from Wikimedia Commons, each credited in `src/photos/credits.ts`.
- Several points of view where historians or the people involved disagree, each quoted from a source (`voices`).
- Sensitive topics (slavery, conquest, colonial violence, war crimes) get a content note, plain wording and no celebrations.
- The unit adds its snapshot(s) to the time map in `src/data/throughTime.ts`.
- Every lesson's `icon` is drawn in `src/screens/Icon.tsx` (`npm test` checks), rather than falling back to the map icon.
- Pictures checked by eye for answers: a document shown with a fill-the-gap must not show the missing word (crop it, or show something else), and no tour stop, caption or alt text may answer a question that follows.
- Where the unit is about how pictures or buildings look, a style sorter teaches the tells (Units 17, 18 and 19), and a building front or label-the-parts question names the parts.
- Where the unit is about something heard (a language, a song, a speech), real recordings from Wikimedia Commons, credited in `src/sounds/credits.ts`, and questions answered by listening (Unit 20; anthems and Radio Oranje in Units 5, 9, 11 and 19).
- Where a feast or a day of remembrance comes up, a when-is-it question on the calendar wheel (Unit 22; Leiden's 3 October in Unit 5, Liberation Day in Unit 11 and Armistice Day in Unit 14).
- Where a unit follows a road, a race or a campaign, a route map to step along and a ride-the-route question that taps its places in order (Unit 23; Napoleon's last campaign in Unit 8 and Market Garden in Unit 11).
- Where pictures tell a story in steps, or people are known for their words, comic panel questions: balloons put in their panels, or panels put in order (Unit 24; the water works in Unit 1 and Bruegel's proverbs in Unit 16). A speech balloon holds only words that were really said, sung or printed; anything else goes in a caption.
- Where a map's shape is the story (an empire, a colony, a voyage), a true-size question or the projection explorer, so Mercator's map doesn't mislead (Unit 25; the Congo in Unit 13 and the crossing to New Amsterdam in Unit 7).
- Where a unit is about places that can be learned by heart (provinces, regions, countries), a map drill that names them only once they are found (Unit 26).
- A new photo of an artwork, person, building or food gets a card in `src/data/collection.ts`, unless it is a perpetrator, a memorial or a site of persecution (`npm test` checks every card is collected somewhere and every unit has one).
- Every question can turn up on its own, in the checkpoint or the placement test, so none may lean on the story or question before it, and a unit needs at least five lessons with questions (`npm test` checks).
